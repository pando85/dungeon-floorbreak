## Why v2 exists

Listener feedback on v0: too robotic; excessive/noisy SFX undesirable; references should be *clear, satisfying fighting-game hits* rather than constant punchy noise; meaningful assessment requires playing/seeing the actual scene; professional sound-design review is appropriate.

Changes in v2:

- Replaced overtly pitched sine-down sweep in primary kick impact with short layered broadband transients (contact, low shoe/body thud, secondary surface slap, cloth movement).
- Reduced the impact clip length 420ms → 330ms; kept the perceived primary strike within roughly the first 100ms.
- Reduced kick whoosh peak from 0.82 → 0.41 and removed its pitched sinusoidal sweep. Swing sound is only a subtle lead-in.
- Shortened body collisions 590ms → 410ms and the wall break 1.2s → 0.91s; debris has fewer, irregular tails.
- Removed the obviously melodic synthetic goblin squeal and substituted a **nonverbal breath-like synthetic placeholder**, not a finished vocal.
- Rebalanced existing Floor 1 music from v0 to reduce bass/hat harshness, with RMS at 0.041 vs v0 0.181 for combat on exported WAVs. These levels are *assets*, not loudness-matched promotional previews.
- Matched the overall energy of the three demo variants and ducked background/music briefly on major hits (~3.2dB peak local ducking).
- Added 3 variations per hit style, whoosh, enemy breath reaction, collision, and concrete break, to mitigate repetition.

This is a sonic sketch, **still generated by DSP rather than recorded material**. It may improve readability but will not sound like professional Foley until source recordings, editing and real-game mixing are added.

## Audio families

| Style | Goal | Sound characteristic |
|---|---|---|
| Arcade | Very legible hit confirmations | Sharp dry transient; slightly compressed, minimal tail |
| Cinematic | Physical body movement | Deeper layered contact/fabric; softer crack |
| Hybrid | THE CRAWL working candidate | Middle of the two: short snap + textured body thud, controlled tail |

All use the same music, ambience, timings, enemy reactions, scene events and post-processing. **The three mix variants do not change the gameplay animation.**

## What not to infer

- Do not mark v2 as approved based on this animatic. The true test is the integrated, interactive Bevy fight with realistic repetition and voice-stealing.
- The animatic synchronizes synthetic combat events but cannot test actual player input latency, hitstop, animation events, enemy physics or listening fatigue.
- There is no completed human-expert review or produced/recorded final goblin/Donut/Mongo vocal performance.
- Nothing here implies we have already opened or modified a GitHub PR.
