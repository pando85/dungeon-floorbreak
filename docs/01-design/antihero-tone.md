# Antihero action identity — mandatory project-wide creative rule

**Status:** Owner-approved direction, 2026-11-15. **Applies to:** narrative, character design, combat, enemies, levels, cinematic language, animation, VFX, SFX, marketing concepts, and every generated asset or AI-agent prompt.

## North star

**Dungeon Floorbreak must feel like a grimy, irreverent 1980s–1990s action movie starring an ordinary, increasingly battered antihero — thrown into an absurd, lethal dungeon and surviving through stubbornness, improvisation, bad luck, explosive ingenuity, and earned charisma.**

This is a **genre/tone reference**, not an invitation to reproduce an existing film, actor, scene, costume, dialogue, poster, soundtrack or likeness.

### Five required qualities

1. **Everyman before action icon.** The protagonist begins believably ordinary: imperfect posture, unremarkable face, natural body proportions, occasional awkwardness. Ability, presence and charisma are **earned over the story**. Do not turn Carl into a conventionally handsome superhero, bodybuilder or power-fantasy soldier.
2. **Scrappy wins, not effortless dominance.** Characters win with traps, unexpected combinations, environmental destruction, companions and willingness to improvise under pressure. Sell effort, hesitations, mishaps, painful recoveries and narrow escapes.
3. **Grit + spectacle + gallows humor.** Dirty utility spaces, bruises, smoke, sweat, sparks, malfunctioning machinery, dramatic explosions and wicked situational comedy. Explosions can be cinematic; consequences and character reactions must remain legible.
4. **The absurd system is part of the antagonist.** Design dungeons and interactions to evoke a cruel entertainment apparatus: theatrical announcements, bureaucratic absurdity, spectacle framing, and dangerous rules. Do not invent specific franchise-canon events or dialogue and label them as canonical.
5. **Heart underneath the sarcasm.** Humor and swagger must never erase fear, fatigue, loyalty, moral costs or the growing responsibility of protecting allies. The protagonist's confident final posture is a narrative payoff, not his starting look.

## Carl visual progression: constraints, not invented canon

| Phase for concept exploration | Face, body and movement | Equipment and expression |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Early survivor** | Ordinary build, slight softness around the waist, modest shoulders, slightly confused or irritated expression, awkward weight shifts | Recognizable canon-appropriate clothing (including boxers and opening-story footwear where accurate); apparently ill-prepared, resourceful |
| **Developing anarchist** | Small gains in functional strength, increased alertness; posture starts to settle into combat readiness, but still human | Add only book-verified gear for the relevant point in the story; visually emphasize improvised solutions |
| **Seasoned veteran** | Mildly more muscular, leaner where appropriate, tired eyes, visible wear and injuries; movement economical, occasionally rough | Equipment is practical, scuffed, and mismatched; confidence conveyed by action rather than physique |
| **Reluctant leader** | Still recognizably the same regular man; no sudden V-shaped torso or heroic jaw; controlled but visibly exhausted stance | Earned authority, scars and continuity of worn gear; no default cape, plate armor or invented superhero uniform |

**Source-of-truth constraint:** These phases are *conceptual buckets*, not precise book-by-book canon. Maintain a separate evidence table by book/floor/chapter for appearance, items, tattoos, hair and unlocks. Label each item `canon verified`, `design interpretation` or `gameplay proposal`. If uncertain, do not present it as fact or add it to a canonical asset.

### Carl art do/don't

- **Do:** imperfect face with ordinary features; slightly disheveled hair; small asymmetric expressions; human shoulders/limbs; realistic center of mass; small incremental changes; practical, battered gear; unglamorous reactions; visible physical effort.
- **Don't:** superhero jawline, six-pack, exaggerated chest/shoulders, glamour lighting on muscles, tactical-action-hero armor by default, perfect combat poses, heroic cape, confident grin in every frame, or a totally different body shape at each stage.
- **At gameplay scale:** preserve a stable silhouette and foot contact; the antihero personality must read through body language before tiny face detail. Start from the documented provisional virtual pixel size and review at 1x, Steam Deck scale and 4K integer output.

## How this tone changes the rest of the game

| Discipline | Required direction | Anti-pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Character animation** | Scrambles, surprise recoil, breath, frustration, imperfect landings; earned fluidity after progression | Superhero three-point landings by default, weightless perfection |
| **Combat and destruction** | Dirty, chunky impacts and environmental solutions; explosive spectacle with clear anticipation/after-effects | Indiscriminate screen-filling particles or unstoppable effortless combos |
| **Levels and props** | Industrial-service dungeon grime, ridiculous signage, improvised hazards, theatrical deadly machinery | Generic pristine medieval-fantasy rooms |
| **Enemies** | Threatening yet eccentric, motivated behaviors and readable tells; some absurd visual contrasts | Purely serious faceless army or comedic enemies with no danger |
| **Narrative and dialogue** | Dry one-liners, tension-and-release, inconvenient setbacks, sincere stakes between jokes | Constant jokes, empty catchphrases or lifted movie/franchise lines |
| **UI** | Rugged show-business/game-show framing with slightly trashy late-analog flair; accessible pixel readability | Slick clean sci-fi superhero UI |
| **Sound and music** | Punchy original action rhythm, crunchy impacts, restrained tense ambience and comic contrast | Direct imitation or reuse of film scores; wall-to-wall bombast |
| **Camera and lighting** | Brief dramatic action emphasis; moody blue-violet dungeons, hot orange explosions and magenta abilities; never obscure controls | Permanent cinematic blur, VHS filtering that muddies pixels, excessive shake |

VHS grain, action-poster motifs, helicopter silhouettes and other period signals are **optional marketing/concept-art shorthand**, not requirements for every dungeon or gameplay frame. The atmosphere should feel 80s/90s through *attitude and stakes* more than nostalgic decoration.

## Review gate for human and AI contributors

Before accepting a design, answer:

1. Would this still look like a **normal person under extraordinary pressure**, not a Marvel-like action hero?
2. Is the action **earned, improvised, and a little messy**?
3. Are danger, dark humor and genuine emotional stakes all present?
4. Does it obey pixel-art readability and material/destruction rules?
5. Are lore details evidenced and permissions/provenance respected?
6. Is it original in expression, without copied third-party film/game/franchise assets?

Reject or revise if (1), (4), (5) or (6) fail; revise tonal staging if (2) or (3) fail. Do not confuse a beautiful concept render with a production-valid pixel sprite.
